Decisions not directly established by the references

  1. Upstream DENT branch: the project moves the voltage/power tap before CT to keep meter self-consumption out of the measured current. This is an engineering adaptation, not the factory figure. Cable voltage drop can still affect the relationship to printer-inlet energy. Review the topology and validate it against a reference meter.
  2. Q0 = 15 A and Qv = 1 A: the products exist (15 A, 1 A), but their suitability has not been established by a load, inrush, fault-current and conductor/protection assessment.
  3. Existing CT: model, output, range and polarity have not been identified. R1 printed p. 9 specifies compatible shunted CTs with a 333 mV maximum output. A generic “Hall effect” description is insufficient to establish compatibility.
  4. Plate and rail bonding hardware: dedicated connections are shown, but actual earth terminals, hardware, conductor compatibility and continuity testing remain unspecified. Ordinary support screws are not an assumed electrical bond.
  5. Physical routing: positions in the drawing are expanded for readability. Wire lengths, bend radii, protection covers, fastening points, complete voltage-lead slack and insulation clearances require a real dry fit. Separate USB routing is a design choice; suitable insulation is still necessary.
